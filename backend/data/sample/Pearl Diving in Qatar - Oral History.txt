Pearl diving was the backbone of Qatar's economy before the discovery of oil.
Every summer, from June to September, the men of Doha, Al Khor, and Al Wakrah would set sail
on their dhows for the pearl banks of the Arabian Gulf.

My grandfather, Mohammed bin Abdullah, was a renowned Nokhatha (captain) who led diving
expeditions for over thirty years. He would tell us stories of the Al Ghous season — how
the entire community would gather at the shore for the departure ceremony. The women would
sing traditional farewell songs, and the air would fill with the scent of oud and bakhoor.

The diving crew consisted of the Ghais (diver) who would descend to the seabed, the Saib
(rope puller) who managed the safety rope, and the Nokhatha who navigated the vessel. Each
diver would attach a stone to his foot, hold his nose with a clip made from turtle shell
called the Fatam, and plunge into the depths holding a mesh basket called the Diyeen.

Life aboard the pearling dhow was harsh. The crew survived on dates, dried fish, rice, and
coffee. Fresh water was precious and carefully rationed. Despite the hardships, there was a
deep sense of brotherhood. In the evenings, the sailors would gather on deck to sing Nahham
— traditional sea songs that echoed across the Gulf waters.

The pearl trade was centered in the souqs of Doha and connected Qatar to markets in Bombay,
Baghdad, and Paris. The Tawwash (pearl merchant) would evaluate each pearl for its size,
luster, shape, and color. The finest pearls from Qatar were known as Jiwan — perfect,
round specimens that commanded extraordinary prices.

The decline of pearl diving began in the 1930s when the Japanese cultured pearl industry
devastated global prices. Combined with the Great Depression, many diving families fell into
debt. The discovery of oil in 1939 at Dukhan gradually provided an alternative livelihood,
marking the end of an era that had defined Qatar's identity for centuries.

Today, the pearl remains a powerful symbol of Qatari heritage. The Pearl Monument on the
Corniche and The Pearl-Qatar island development pay tribute to this defining chapter of
the nation's history.
