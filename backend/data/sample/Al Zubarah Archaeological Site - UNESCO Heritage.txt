Al Zubarah is Qatar's only UNESCO World Heritage Site, inscribed in 2013. Located on
the northwestern coast of Qatar, this remarkable archaeological site tells the story of one
of the most important trading and pearl diving towns in the Gulf during the 18th and early
19th centuries.

Founded around 1760, Al Zubarah quickly grew into a prosperous trading hub that rivaled
established ports across the Gulf. The town was established by merchants from Kuwait,
particularly from the Al Khalifa family, who were drawn by the rich pearl banks nearby
and the strategic location for maritime trade.

At its peak, Al Zubarah had a population of approximately 6,000 people and was protected
by an outer wall with semicircular towers and a screening wall along the seafront. The town
featured a well-planned layout with a souq (marketplace), mosques, courtyard houses, and
a sophisticated water management system using wells and channels.

The town's economy was built on pearl diving, fishing, and long-distance maritime trade.
Merchants from Al Zubarah traded with ports across the Indian Ocean — from East Africa to
South Asia. Archaeological excavations have uncovered Chinese porcelain, Japanese ceramics,
South Asian beads, and European manufactured goods, testifying to the town's far-reaching
trade connections.

Al Zubarah was destroyed in 1811 following a military campaign by the Sultanate of Oman.
The Al Zubarah Fort, which still stands today, was built in 1938 during the reign of
Sheikh Abdullah bin Jassim Al Thani and served as a coast guard station. It has been
restored and now functions as a museum.

The archaeological excavations, led by the Qatar Museums Authority in collaboration with
the University of Copenhagen, have revealed extraordinary details about life in the Gulf
before the oil era. The site provides unmatched evidence of the urban planning, architecture,
and economic networks that characterized the Gulf's pearling economy.

The inscription of Al Zubarah as a UNESCO World Heritage Site recognizes its Outstanding
Universal Value as a testimony to the urban trading and pearl diving tradition that sustained
the major coastal towns of the Gulf region.
